UAV Payload Bracket – Structural Optimization

Load-path-driven bracket design and FEA-based structural optimization.

Overview

Design and structural optimization of a UAV payload support bracket using FEA-driven geometry refinement in Creo. The project focuses on improving load transfer efficiency while minimizing excess material.

Load Configuration & Design Challenge

The following section illustrates the structural configuration of the bracket and the resulting load distribution derived from initial payload calculations.

Load Assumptions and Distribution

  • Given payload mass: 100 kg
  • Equivalent static load: ~981 N (F = m·g)
  • Applied load amplification factor: ~3× (to account for dynamic effects and safety)
  • Total design load: ~3000 N
  • Load distributed across three inclined struts based on geometry

The applied load is transferred to the bracket through three supporting struts arranged in a non-symmetrical configuration. Each strut is inclined at approximately 65° relative to the mounting platform, resolving the load into combined vertical and lateral components. Due to the unequal load distribution across the struts, the bracket experiences a non-uniform force condition, which directly influences the resulting stress behavior and motivates the need for structural optimization.

Initial Design & Overdesign

Based on the defined loading conditions, an initial bracket design was developed with a conservative uniform thickness to ensure structural integrity during early stages of the design process.

../assets/images/Bracket/initial-design.jpg

Simulation results show a maximum stress of approximately 28 MPa, which is significantly lower than the material yield strength (~500 MPa). This indicates that the structure is overdesigned, containing excess material and unnecessary weight.

Load Path–Driven Geometry

Material was selectively redistributed to follow primary load paths, resulting in a rib-supported structure. Low-stress regions were reduced, while high-stress areas were reinforced to efficiently carry applied loads.

Final Optimized Design

The final geometry integrates rib-supported features and optimized thickness distribution, resulting in an efficient structure capable of handling the applied loads while minimizing weight.

../assets/images/Bracket/final-design.jpg

Simulation Results

../assets/images/Bracket/final-stress.jpg
  • Maximum stress: ~110 MPa
  • Material yield strength: ~500 MPa
  • Factor of Safety: ~4.5

Stress concentrations occur at connection interfaces and rib intersections, which are expected due to load transfer conditions. The stress levels remain well within safe limits, confirming the structural integrity of the design.

Conclusion

The bracket design was successfully optimized through iterative FEA, reducing excess material while maintaining structural integrity. The final geometry achieves an efficient balance between strength and weight under multi-directional loading conditions.
