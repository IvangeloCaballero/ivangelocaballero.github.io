UAV Payload Bracket – Structural Optimization

Load-path-driven bracket design and FEA-based structural optimization.

Overview

Design and structural optimization of a UAV payload support bracket using FEA-driven geometry refinement in Creo. The project focuses on improving load transfer efficiency while minimizing excess material.

Load Configuration & Design Challenge

The following section illustrates the structural configuration of the bracket and the resulting load distribution derived from initial payload calculations.

Load Assumptions and Distribution

  • Given payload mass: 100 kg
  • Equivalent static load: ~981 N (F = m·g)
  • Applied load amplification factor: ~3× (to account for dynamic effects and safety)
  • Total design load: ~3000 N
  • Load distributed across three inclined struts based on geometry

The applied load is transferred to the bracket through three supporting struts arranged in a non-symmetrical configuration. Each strut is inclined at approximately 65° relative to the mounting platform, resolving the load into combined vertical and lateral components. Due to the unequal load distribution across the struts, the bracket experiences a non-uniform force condition, which directly influences the resulting stress behavior and motivates the need for structural optimization.

The bracket supports a payload through three inclined struts, creating a multi-directional load condition. Due to the asymmetrical strut arrangement, load distribution is non-uniform, requiring a geometry that efficiently transfers forces to the mounting interface while minimizing unnecessary material.

  • Working Fluid: Water (clean / lightly contaminated)
  • Flow Rate (Design): 3 m³/hr — selected to maintain manageable pipe velocities while aligning with compact system sizing
  • Operating Pressure: 6 bar — assumed to account for pumping requirements and system resistance through filtration components.
  • Design Pressure: 12 bar
  • Temperature Range: Ambient conditions (25–35°C)

Industry-standard codes were referenced to support realistic implementation. Piping and components were aligned with ASME B31.3 guidelines, with consistent use of ASME B16.5 Class 300 ratings across primary connections.

Material selection and sizing decisions were made to balance structural integrity, safety margins, and practical fabrication considerations. These assumptions served as the foundation for layout development, component integration, and cost estimation.

Conceptual Layout and Process Flow

The conceptual floor plan illustrates the initial spatial arrangement of major components within the skid, focusing on placement, routing feasibility, and accessibility for operation and maintenance. Adequate spacing between equipment was considered to allow service access, inspection, and ease of installation. The process flow diagram complements this by defining the sequence of operation from inlet through pumping, filtration, and distribution, providing a clear representation of system functionality and flow direction.

System Layout Development

The layout was refined by iterating component positioning to improve accessibility and piping efficiency. Orthographic and isometric views were used to verify clearances, routing feasibility, and overall integration before finalizing the skid arrangement.

Component Design and Standard Integration

The system incorporates both custom-designed components and standard parts. Valves and piping elements were selected from Inventor Content Center to ensure compatibility with commonly used industrial components. Major equipment such as the filtration unit, storage tanks, pump configuration, and supporting skid structure were modeled to meet system requirements and spatial constraints.

  • Custom modeling of tanks, filter unit, pump setup, control panel, pipe support and skid frame
  • Use of Inventor Content Center for standard valves and piping
  • Consideration of component spacing and routing to allow service access and maintenance
  • Consistent use of Class 300 flanges for NPS 1 and NPS 3 piping ensures standardization and provides a conservative pressure margin relative to the 6 bar operating condition.

Piping and custom components were designed in accordance with ASME B16.5 Class 300 standards to ensure dimensional compatibility, correct bolt alignment, and reliable sealing across all flange connections.

Reference: ASME B16.5 Class 300 flange tables

Custom Component Design

Custom components were modeled to match system requirements while maintaining realistic proportions, connection interfaces, and manufacturability considerations.

Bill of Materials and Cost Estimation

A bill of materials (BOM) was developed to quantify all major system components, including equipment, piping, structural elements, and supports. This formed the basis for an overall cost estimate and feasibility assessment.

  • Total Project Cost: ₱216,492.75
  • Primary Cost Drivers: Equipment, Labor, and Fabrication

Key Observation: Equipment and labor together account for nearly 50% of total project cost, indicating that both procurement and installation effort significantly influence system budgeting.

Piping and structural elements contribute secondary costs, while contingency provides an allowance for uncertainty in early-stage estimation, resulting in a more robust overall project budget.
