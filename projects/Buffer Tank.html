Buffer Tank - SolidWorks Simulation

Concept‑level pressure vessel design with structural evaluation of nozzle interfaces.

Overview

This project presents a concept‑level buffer tank design developed to study shell sizing, nozzle geometry, and structural behavior under internal pressure using static analysis.
The geometry was reconstructed from publicly available reference images of generic industrial buffer tanks for concept‑level evaluation.

Objective

Evaluate structural behavior and safety margin under assumed operating and maximum internal pressure, with emphasis on identifying governing regions.

Key Parameters

  • Material: ASTM A36 Steel
  • Shell thickness: 6 mm
  • Operating pressure: 0.2 MPa
  • Maximum pressure: 0.6 MPa
  • Evaluation: von Mises stress & yield‑based FoS

Structural Results

Structural response is governed by the maximum internal pressure case (0.6 MPa). Comparison of operating (0.2 MPa) and maximum internal pressure cases shows von Mises stress and yield‑based Factor of Safety concentrated at nozzle–shell junctions, with minimum FoS ≈ 2.4 at maximum pressure.
The operating pressure case exhibits a significantly higher safety margin and does not govern design decisions.

Image gallery

Conclusion

Based on the concept‑level analysis, the buffer tank remains structurally acceptable at the assumed maximum internal pressure of 0.6 MPa, with the nozzle–shell junctions clearly governing the structural response and a minimum Factor of Safety of about 2.4. To improve margin for higher or maximum pressure operation, I would increase the shell thickness from the current 6 mm to roughly 8–10 mm. In addition, I would recommend adding local reinforcement pads around the nozzle flanges—on the order of 1.0–1.5 times the shell thickness and approximately 2–3 times the nozzle diameter—to better distribute load and reduce local stress concentration.
