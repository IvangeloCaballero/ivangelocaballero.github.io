Buffer Tank - Pressure Vessel Analysis

Concept‑level pressure vessel design with structural evaluation of nozzle interfaces.

Overview

This project presents a concept‑level buffer tank design developed to evaluate shell sizing, nozzle geometry, and structural behavior under internal pressure using static analysis.
The geometry was reconstructed from publicly available reference images of generic industrial buffer tanks for concept‑level evaluation.

Engineering Analysis

  • Internal pressure conditions were defined as the primary loading case for structural evaluation.
  • The maximum pressure case was treated as the governing condition for structural evaluation and design validation.
  • Shell thickness (6 mm) was determined to maintain stresses within acceptable limits under operating conditions.
  • Stress distribution was evaluated using FEA, with focus on nozzle openings (Ø160–Ø300 range) and geometric discontinuities.
  • Tank volume (~250–300 L) was defined based on process requirements.
  • Nozzle placement was configured to support flow requirements while minimizing stress concentration.

Key Parameters

  • Material: AISI A36 Steel
  • Shell thickness: 6 mm
  • Operating pressure: 0.2 MPa
  • Maximum pressure: 0.6 MPa
  • Evaluation: von Mises stress & yield‑based Factor of Safety

Structural Results

Structural response is governed by the maximum internal pressure case (0.6 MPa). Comparison of operating (0.2 MPa) and maximum internal pressure cases shows von Mises stress and yield‑based Factor of Safety concentrated at nozzle–shell junctions, with minimum FoS ≈ 2.4.
The operating pressure case exhibits a significantly higher safety margin and does not govern design decisions.

Fabrication Drawing and Detail Views

Conclusion

Based on the concept‑level analysis, the buffer tank remains structurally acceptable at the assumed maximum internal pressure of 0.6 MPa, with the nozzle–shell junctions governing the response and a minimum Factor of Safety of about 2.4. To improve margin for higher pressure operation, increasing shell thickness from 6 mm to approximately 8–10 mm is recommended. Additional reinforcement pads around nozzle flanges would further reduce stress concentration and improve durability.
