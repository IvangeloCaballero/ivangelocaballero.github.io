Motor Wiper Linkage - Mechanism & Motion Design

End‑to‑end mechanical design and documentation case study.

Overview

Design a motor‑driven wiper linkage capable of converting rotary motor input into controlled, synchronized output motion. The focus was on defining correct linkage geometry, motion constraints, and component interfaces to support accurate kinematics and production‑ready documentation.

Design Considerations

  • Link lengths were selected to achieve approximately 75°-80° wiper sweep while avoiding interference between moving components
  • Pivot locations were adjusted to maintain smooth motion and reduce binding across the full range.
  • A minimum clearance of approximately 5 mm was maintained between moving links and adjacent components to prevent collision during operation.

Design Process

A complete linkage concept was developed through parametric CAD modeling and kinematic assembly definition. Components were modeled from scratch, assembled with motion constraints, and refined to verify smooth operation and logical assembly order prior to documentation release.

  • Functional requirements analysis & linkage architecture definition
  • Linkage geometry definition & dimensional layout validation based on target angular sweep
  • Concept sketches and CAD exploration to refine spatial arrangement and mechanism configuration
  • Motion behavior review & clearance checks across full sweep range

Final Outcome

The final result is a fully defined wiper linkage assembly with validated kinematic behavior and complete manufacturing documentation. The design includes clear part relationships, an organized BOM, and an exploded assembly view suitable for communication with manufacturing, supplier quotation, and further development or program integration.
